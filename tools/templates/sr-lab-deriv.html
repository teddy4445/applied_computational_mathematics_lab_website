Simple math

A weight on a spring, filmed by a phone

The page simulates four recordings of 6 seconds (starts of 2 to 5 cm) from a = −16·x − 0.4·v, our own made-up spring. The camera sees the position x only, with tracking noise. Every pipeline searches the same library (1, x, v, x², x·v, x³, x²·v) with thresholded least squares. Everything runs in your browser.

Finite differences
a ≈ (xᵢ₊₁ − 2xᵢ + xᵢ₋₁) / Δt²

Simple, and the noise is divided by Δt²: faster cameras make it worse.

Smoothing
local cubic, then differentiate

Less noise, but the window also flattens the swing: a bias.

Weak form
∫ φ·a dt = ∫ φ″·x dt

A bump φ that vanishes at both ends takes the derivatives; no noisy derivative is computed.

Challenges

Each one is checked as you go.

  1. Find a noise level where finite differences lose the right terms but the weak form keeps them.
  2. Show that a faster camera makes the finite-difference acceleration noisier (same tracking noise, higher frame rate).
  3. Pick a smoothing window that pushes the damping error above 30% while the weak form stays under 5%.
  4. Record one new swing that brings the stiffening coefficient within 3% of 400.
