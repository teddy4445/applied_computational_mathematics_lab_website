Simple math

A plant on the coast, one field law

The page simulates a plant spreading along 100 km of coast with the Fisher–KPP equation: ∂u/∂t = 0.5·u·(1 − u) + 4·∂²u/∂x², where u is the fraction of ground covered. Sensors every 1 km read u four times a week for 24 weeks, with the noise you choose; the ends of the coast are closed. Everything runs in your browser.

A library of field terms
u, u², u³, u_x, u_xx, u·u_x, u_xxx

Growth and crowding, drift, spread, transport that depends on cover, and a wave term.

The weak form
∬ φ·∂u/∂t = −∬ ∂φ/∂t·u

A smooth bump on windows 12 km × 3 weeks takes every derivative; the readings are only integrated.

two releases that merge

The found law runs on an experiment it never saw; the error is in cover, 0 to 1.

Challenges

Each one is checked as you go.

  1. Find the plant’s three-term law at 1% noise or more. Hint: the pipeline matters.
  2. Watch one front through the 40-km window and get a law with a drift term; then find the plant’s law from the middle release, with the same noise and method.
  3. Find where the weak form stops working on the middle release: the law found at 1% noise and missed at 2%.
  4. Forecast the merging fronts to within 0.005 of cover (RMS) with a law found at 1% noise or more.
