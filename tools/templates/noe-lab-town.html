Simple math

Town in silico: 300 firms, nobody told to hide

A small version of the videos’ Saltmarsh. Every quarter each invented firm chooses how much of its cash sales to declare, weighing the tax, the audit risk it believes, a moral cost that grows with trust, and what its peer group does. Card sales are always recorded. Trust follows the revenue the town collects.

A firm’s choice
max −τd − p̂(τ+φ)(1−d) − m(1−d)

Declared share d; believed audit chance p̂; moral cost m.

Moral cost
m = m₀(0.4 + trust) − peers × group hiding

Peers make hiding cheaper; trust makes it dearer.

Feedback
trust ← trust + 0.25(services − trust)

Services are paid by the revenue actually collected.

Challenges

Each one is checked on your current settings.

  1. Find an audit rate where a town that starts with hiding common ends at least 5 points above one that starts honest.
  2. Switch on the recession and see hiding still at least a point higher two years after demand returns.
  3. Switch on e-invoicing and see the small firms, which were not reformed, hide at least 8 points less.
  4. Get the hidden share below 15% with an audit rate of at most 8%.
