Simple math

A light ball falls through air

Its speed is logged every 0.05 s for 3.5 s. The truth in this simulation is quadratic drag with k₂ = 0.12 per metre (made up, roughly a ping-pong ball). You only see noisy readings. Everything runs in your browser.

A flexible curve
v(t) = c₀ + c₁t + … + c_d t^d

Least squares on the readings inside the window. More degrees, more freedom.

Linear drag
dv/dt = g − k₁ v

Solved exactly and fitted by one number, k₁, in units of one over seconds.

Quadratic drag
dv/dt = g − k₂ v|v|

Also one number, k₂, in units of one over metres. Its curve levels off at √(g/k₂).

Challenges

Each one is checked on your current settings.

  1. With a window of 1 s or less, find a polynomial that fits the window better than quadratic drag, yet misses the future by more than ten times as much.
  2. With sensor noise of at least 0.10 m/s, get the fitted k₂ within 5% of the true 0.12 per metre.
  3. Make the polynomial predict impossible physics: a negative speed, or more than 30 m/s, somewhere in the 3.5 s.
  4. Kepler: find a polynomial that fits the six planets to within 1%, yet misses Uranus by more than 20%, while the power law stays within 2%.
