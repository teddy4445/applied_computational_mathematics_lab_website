Simple math

A chemical clock, one sparse law

The page simulates a chemical clock with the Brusselator, a textbook model of oscillating reactions: dX/dt = 1 − (b+1)·X + X²·Y and dY/dt = b·X − X²·Y, where b is the feed of the second chemical. Both concentrations are read every 3 seconds for 15 minutes, from up to six starting mixtures, with the noise you choose. Everything runs in your browser.

Challenges

Each one is checked as you go.

  1. Recover the clock’s five-term law at 1% noise or more. Hint: one starting mixture isn’t enough.
  2. Show that coverage matters: with the same noise, method and threshold, one run settled on the cycle fails where six starting mixtures succeed.
  3. Find the threshold window at 1% noise with the weak form: one threshold that keeps more than ten terms, one that keeps fewer than five, and one that finds the law.
  4. Put the feed in the library and find a law whose onset is within 0.05 of b = 2 and whose period at b = 4 is within 3% of the truth.
