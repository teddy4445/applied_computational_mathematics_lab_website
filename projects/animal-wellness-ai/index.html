About the project

What this project is about

Analyzing behavioral and sensorial data to derive insights into animal health and welfare.

This project studies animal behavior and welfare through data-driven sensing and computational ethology. The lab uses video, landmarks, vocalizations, kinematics, and physiological signals to detect pain, stress, social dynamics, and environmental effects in companion, captive, and farm animals. Across species, the shared goal is to extract practical welfare indicators from non-invasive observations while also learning more about communication and behavior.

Timeline

How the project developed.

  1. 06/2023

    Program launched

    The lab consolidated its animal-AI work around welfare assessment, facial signaling, and non-invasive sensing.

  2. 2024

    Computer-vision expansion

    Multiple projects targeted cats, dogs, cattle, and insect larvae with visual and multimodal pipelines.

  3. 2025-2026

    Behavior and social-communication studies

    The project deepened into chimpanzee social ranking, cat facial signaling, and broader welfare-oriented analyses.
