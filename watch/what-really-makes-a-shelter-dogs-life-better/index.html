What really makes a shelter dog's life better?

For four weeks a small telemetry device under the skin recorded heart rate, muscle activity and body temperature in eight male dogs living long-term in an Italian shelter, with a different enrichment each week: toys and objects, the company of female dogs, or a person. New welfare indicators measured changes from a baseline day, sleep quality and day-night rhythms. All three enrichments improved welfare, individual dogs varied a lot, and the company of female dogs worked best for sleep and daily rhythms. Eight dogs in one shelter.

Based on the paper

Environmental enrichments and data-driven welfare indicators for sheltered dogs using telemetric physiological measures and signal processing

Scientific Reports, 2024
