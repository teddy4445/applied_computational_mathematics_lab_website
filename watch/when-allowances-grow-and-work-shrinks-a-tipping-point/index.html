When allowances grow and work shrinks: a tipping point

Our differential equation model follows working people whose taxes fund public goods and the allowances of a group that stays out of the labor market, applied to allowances for ultra-Orthodox men in Israel who devote their time to Torah study. Bigger allowances raise the group's private consumption, but fewer workers then fund public goods, well-being falls, and eventually the group's own benefits shrink; past a critical allowance, public goods and well-being break down. A model of economic utility only.

Based on the paper

Got much, got nothing: analyzing the impact of increased special interest groups’ influence on utility

Eurasian Economic Review, 2025
