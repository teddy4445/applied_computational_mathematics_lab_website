Computational Approaches to the Non-Observed Economy

An open course on studying the non-observed economy with computation: what goes unrecorded and why, games between observers and the observed, estimating and forecasting a hidden quantity with honest errors, maps and networks, agent-based simulation, and policy design. 9 modules, one per chapter of the forthcoming book, with long videos, slides and browser labs.

The course book. Computational Approaches to the Non-Observed Economy: From Theory to Practice. Coming soon. Each module follows one chapter of the book; the videos, slides and labs are written in our own words, figures and simulations, with invented towns and countries.

How to study each module

Every module page brings the same three things together. Use them in any order; this one works well.

Watch

Two or three animated videos per module, 8 to 11 minutes each, drawn on a night map as they go, with a transcript to read along. Many pause for you to predict a result first, and every one works through examples with our own numbers.

Read the slides

Each deck is written to be read on your own: worked examples, our own small simulations, common pitfalls, and check-yourself questions with answers at the end. The matching chapter of the book goes deeper.

Try it

Every module has a lab that runs in your browser, starting with Lantern Bay’s observers in Module 1. The challenges are checked as you go.

Labs

The labs run the course’s ideas in your browser, with challenges that are checked as you go. One lab per module; new ones appear as modules go online.

Lantern Bay's observers

Observers, components, definitions and denominators in an invented town.

Module 1 lab

Forty countries

Random versus country splits, estimate versus truth, and a reform that changes what is observed.

Module 2 lab

The audit game

An inspection game with learning firms, and tipping when people copy each other.

Module 3 lab

Estimate the Archipelago

Linear models, trees and neighbours on invented countries, with ranges you can test.

Module 4 lab

The forecast desk

Persistence, regressions and anchor-and-update through three kinds of drift, checked honestly.

Module 5 lab

The night atlas

Neighbours, smoothing, borders, Moran's I and false hot spots on an invented map.

Module 6 lab

Town in silico

Peer effects, trust feedback, recessions, reforms and tipping points in an invented town.

Module 7 lab

The policy room

Criteria under uncertainty, a Pareto front and the value of a pilot.

Module 8 lab

Study designer

A self-checking study card, and selection optimism with and without nesting.

Module 9 lab

Advancing computational mathematics through real-world problem solving.
